§AWhat ships today

Tap a row. The overlay cross-fades in over 180ms — scrim and card as one flat image, the card never travelling — and the close is a hard cut, because onClose unmounts the portal on the frame it runs.

This is the shipped .doc-viewer CSS inlined from screens.css, not a reconstruction. At 1× the entrance is easy to miss entirely, which is why the report reads it as "no animation".

SPEED does not slow this panel down, and that is the diagnosis rather than a lab bug: the control scales --t-quick and --t-base, and the shipped rule is a literal (animation: fade 0.18s ease) that reads neither. A surface off the ramp cannot be re-tuned from the ramp.

§BThe proposal — all three beats

Scrim and card on separate channels, the card scaling from .94 with --ease-arrive and its origin on the row you tapped; an exit at --t-quick playing the same keyframes reversed; the frame held still and the image cross-fading in.

The origin is transform-origin: 50% calc(50% + var(--dv-origin-dy)) — one number read off the tapped row, no card measurement, no --dir. Open the last row and then the first, and watch where the card grows from.

Look at the dark stage once it settles. The fixed frame letterboxes, which a content-sized frame never did — so the strip beside the scan is now a visible decision, and today it is --paper, which in dark is a warm #2e2a20 and reads as a stain rather than a mount. Candidates are keeping --paper or using --board. Left open here, but it has to be answered before the fixed frame ships, because the fixed frame is what makes it visible.

§CSide by side, same tap, same frame

The comparison the report is actually about. Both phones open the same document at the same instant, in the same theme. Left is today, right is the proposal.

The two cards are different heights on purpose and it is not a drawing error: the proposal's frame floor is holding the right-hand card at its full size (measured 468px against 406px) where today's is sized by whatever landed in it. That difference is part of the proposal, not a confound in the panel.

§DBeat 3 alone — the pop nobody filed

Set NETWORK to cold fetch above, then open both. Left is beats 1 and 2 only: the overlay arrives and leaves properly, and then the card still snaps taller when the bytes land and the image appears with no transition. Right adds the stable frame and the opacity cross-fade.

Measured: +253px on a 564px frame — nearly half the screen, and about 5× the distance the card travels during its own arrival. This is the beat that is specific to documents rather than to overlays, and it is why "beats 1 and 2 only" is a worse deal than it sounds: it fixes the two movements you notice and leaves the big one in. §EOrigin — from the tapped row, or summoned at centre

Both are the full proposal; only --dv-origin-dy differs. Open the last row in each, at 4×, and the difference is legible: on the left the card grows out of the thing you pressed, on the right it appears in the middle of the screen with no connection to it.

Centre is the honest fallback rather than a rejected alternative — it is what a note deep-link (?doc=, ADR-0153 §8) gets, because there is no row to come from. So both behaviours ship; the question is only which is the default when a row was tapped.

§FThe scrim — the visible half of the Wave-2 question

The backlog says these families should fold onto Modal in ADR-0079's Wave 2. Most of that decision is invisible; this part is not. The viewer's own scrim is 0.72, Modal's is 0.45 — and the viewer's is arguably deliberate, because you are inspecting an image and everything behind it is glare.

Judge it in dark especially: on a dark screen a 0.72 scrim over an already dark --screen nearly erases the context, where in light it is the difference between "a document is open" and "a card is floating".

The rest of the fold-on cost is not a drawing: Modal's dialog variant is max-width: 340px with padding: var(--space-4) against the viewer's 480px edge-to-edge, and frontend/index.html:17 hardcodes .doc-viewer as the root of ADR-0062's zoom exception — so folding on means keeping that selector alive or moving it. ADR-0144's precedent is the alternative: reuse the mechanism (useExitTransition), keep the shape.

§GReduced motion is a correctness case

Turn REDUCE on in the bar and close the viewer. The exit introduces a closing state that exists only during an animation — so when there is no animation it has to resolve anyway, or the viewer never unmounts and sits there holding focus. The log below prints the wait the timer actually read.

motionDurationMs already answers 0 here, and useExitTransition already closes synchronously rather than on a 0ms timer. Nothing new is needed — but it is the one thing that must be verified rather than assumed, because a mistake in it is a dead app and no unit test in jsdom can see it (there is no stylesheet there, so every test exercises the zero branch by default).
