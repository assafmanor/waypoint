Dark mode, and what it found in light mode

The dark remap was already complete and completely inert: tokens.css carried every value and nothing in the tree ever set data-theme, so no one had seen it. Setting it on the running app is what this session did. Two of the remap's own recorded conventions turn out to be wrong (§3, §4), the trip chrome has an unenumerated dependency on being dark (§7), and auditing both themes together found four contrast failures in the shipped light theme and none in dark (§5) — each theme was hiding the other's bug class.

§1 The ration: what makes the board loud when everything is dark

Light mode makes the board loud by luminance contrast against paper — it is the only dark thing on screen. Dark mode deletes that mechanism. The remap's stated answer (design-language) is that the board keeps hierarchy by owning the darkest surface while cards sit on lighter dark ones.

Light — the reference The board is the only dark surface, and its edge is unmissable.

Dark — the remap as written Board #0A1120 on screen #0F1726 is ΔL* 2.6, which is not a visible edge. Note the tab bar: it stayed white (§5).

Dark — with §3/§4 fixed ships The board has no boundary against the body and does not need one: every amber thing on the screen is inside it.

What the render corrected. design-language says the board "owns the darkest surface … while cards sit on lighter dark surfaces". Measured, that step is ΔL* 2.6 against 84.8 in light. It is real in the token table and does essentially no work on screen.
What actually carries it: amber density. The clock, the label, the countdown, the platform numeral, the progress bar, the blip — every amber element is inside the board, so the eye goes there with no luminance edge involved. The hierarchy inverts rather than weakening: in light the board is the object and the cards are quiet; in dark the cards are the objects and the board is the field they sit above. That is closer to what the board is — the thing that speaks, not a thing you pick up.
The rule this sharpens. In light a stray amber accent elsewhere is survivable because the board's darkness still separates it. In dark, amber density IS the ration, so amber spent anywhere else comes straight out of the board's prominence with nothing to compensate. "Amber = time" stops being only a semantic rule and becomes a hierarchy rule.

§2 Mode identity, all four combinations

ADR-0028 is a non-negotiable: mode must be readable from the chrome alone, before any content, on at least two channels. Light mode clears that bar by a mile for a reason the rule never names — trip chrome is dark and plan chrome is light, so the modes differ in luminance before anything else. Dark mode deletes that channel. Both modes are captured from the running app.

Trip · light Indigo chrome, amber energy, the pulsing blip.

Plan · light Light drafting chrome, violet hero, drafting grid, no pulse. Unmistakably a different mode.

Plan · dark, AS THE REMAP SHIPS defect The hero fills from --plan-deep, whose dark value was chosen "used as text — lighter on dark". It is also a fill. L* 68 — the brightest surface in the dark app, in the calm mode, ink at 2.16:1.

Plan · dark, fixed ships A deep violet night surface — plan's counterpart of the night board. Ink clears 12.7:1.

The defect is not cosmetic — it inverts mode identity. Trip is supposed to be the loud mode and plan the calm one. As the remap ships, plan mode is the brightest thing in the app and out-shouts the trip board completely.
Why the fix is surface tokens and not a nudged hex. The root cause is an asymmetry: the board has dedicated surface tokens (--board/--board-2) while the plan hero paints itself out of the accent tokens. An accent brightens on dark; a surface must not. Giving plan the same treatment keeps dark mode a token remap and restores the luminance channel the right way round — trip = near-black with amber inside, plan = a lifted violet slab with white on it.

§3 The on-fill ink convention is wrong, and it breaks ten controls

App.css and screens.css both record the same rule from the U-08 sweep: "ink that rides a semantic FILL — the fill re-maps, the on-fill ink must not." True for a fill that keeps its polarity (--amber stays light, --indigo stays dark); false for every mid-tone one, because those were brightened for text legibility and white ink then stops working.

One pair, generalised from the one that already existed. Rule 8 says extend the one-off doing this job rather than adding a second. That one-off is --cta/--cta-text — ADR-0028 already wrote "CTA is a pair … they flip together (dark: light button, dark text)" and then applied it to exactly one token. --on-fill is that same ink named for every fill: #FFFFFF light, #12203A dark. --cta-text becomes an alias. --on-amber takes the other case — the fill that is light in both themes, so its ink stays dark in both.
The fill that must NOT join the family: --indigo. It is always-dark chrome, so it keeps light ink in both themes — dark ink on it measures 1.01:1. Two different rules were written down as one, which is exactly how this shipped.

§4 The light theme has four failures of its own

This was meant to be a dark-mode session. Auditing both themes together inverted the result: text contrast fails four times in the shipped LIGHT theme and zero times in dark — the remap brightened everything and fixed them by accident. Symmetrically, on-fill ink fails ten times in dark and zero in light.

Light, today 4 failures --amber-deep times 3.13 (needs 4.5) · --muted on the ground 3.88 · --faint 2.18.

Light, corrected ships 5.49 · 4.50 · 3.03. Scalar darkenings along the same RGB ray, so hue and saturation are mathematically untouched.

Why --amber itself is not touched. The signature amber #E9A63C lives on the dark board at 8.51:1. Only --amber-deep — amber's paper variant, carrying 10–12.5px bold times across zone chips, leg badges, the glance rail and the tz-shift pill — moves. The product's signature colour is unchanged and rule 4's budget is untouched: same hue, legible depth.

And one bug only a real render could catch: .nav is rgba(255,255,255,.92). The U-08 sweep converted hexes, so it never saw this, and the tab bar stays white in dark mode — visible in §1's middle frame.

The pattern under all of it. Every one of these is a token whose value was chosen for one of its two roles. --amber-deep was chosen as an accent and used as small text. --plan-deep was remapped as text and used as a fill. The fix is always the same: give the second role its own token — which --amber-ink already did once, and nobody generalised.

§5 The chrome assumes it is dark — 53 declarations, 4 marked

You spotted the avatar still carrying the indigo hue on a light chrome. It is not alone. Sweeping every colour declaration on a trip-chrome surface finds 53, of which only 4 carry a fixed: marker. They are not wrong today — they are correct given a dark chrome, and nothing records that dependency.

The good news, and it decides the shape of the fix: plan mode already solved every one of these. When plan's chrome went light it re-specified the lot — .app[data-mode='plan'] .av { border-color: var(--card) } is literally the fix for the ring you spotted, and there are eight more beside it. So a light trip chrome is not new design. But hand-writing it would be the third copy of "how chrome children look on a given ground", which is the shape rule 8 exists to stop — and ADRs 0078, 0079, 0094 and 0095 all exist only to undo copies that piled up like this.
So the fix is to give the chrome a token contract — five names (--chrome-bg, --chrome-ink, --chrome-ink-dim/-mid, --chrome-wash/-2, --chrome-ring) set once per mode. Trip-dark keeps exactly today's values, plan-light collapses to setting the same five, and the light-chrome option in §6 then costs seven lines instead of nineteen rules. This is worth doing whether or not §6 ever ships, because it is what makes the dependency recorded instead of rediscovered.

§6 The light chrome — the open question

Light mode is ~220px of dark chrome above a dark hero, so it reads as half dark-mode. Four renders established that the hero is load-bearing (lighten it and the chrome inherits the prominence, inverting the hierarchy) and the chrome is what looks out of place. These are the grounds, all on the tokenised contract from §5, hero untouched.

1 · warm cream the yellow Cream ground + amber day pill + amber hero accents stack into one yellow field. Also spends --paper ("warm badge accent") on a whole surface.

2 · cool paper Calm and not yellow, but no trip hue left in the chrome at all — the mode-identity cost in its purest form.

3 · indigo 12% recommended Light, cool, and still visibly the trip blue — keeps ADR-0028's hue channel while dropping the luminance one.

4 · indigo 24% Strongest identity read, but it starts to become a third surface competing with the hero — the thing this option exists to stop.

Recommendation: indigo 12%, and not in this branch. It answers the complaint and keeps the trip hue, so trip-vs-plan stays a blue-vs-violet contrast at equal lightness rather than losing its second channel. But it moves a surface that ADR-0028 names as mode identity, that ADR-0142's trip birth animates (indigo → violet) and that ADR-0143's invite pass is choreographed against, and it makes the two theme-fixed dark screens in §7 more anomalous, not less. The backlog already has a Hero 2.0 line for redesigning this exact region; this belongs there, with plan mode re-checked on a device.
What is still unverified, stated plainly. These are Chromium renders at 411×914, not a phone. ADR-0125 is this repo's own precedent for a palette that measured fine and read as one hue on real glass, and the map epic has had four device corrections of things that were derived, tested and wrong.

§7 /login and /join — theme-fixed, and marked

Both are full-screen departure boards today, deliberately: the pre-login brand impression, and design-language's one stated exception to the ration. Their ground is --board-2 → --board, which does re-map, so they already shift by a few L* with no wiring at all.

/login — light theme The board is the whole field. Nothing paper on this screen for it to be scarce against.

/login — dark theme ships Ground darkened, amber brightened, the ~21 fixed inks stayed. That is the entire delta, and it is correct.

They do not theme, and the argument is not "there is no preference to read". A device-local pick is readable on /login, so that argument dissolves. The real one is that these screens are already at the dark end of the axis — theming them means giving them a light state they have never had and do not want, and the only other reading ("the ground goes a few L* darker") is what the tokens already do for free.

They are the same surface class as the indigo header, the dormant board and the boarding-pass ticket: always-dark chrome, every one of which the U-08 sweep already marked. App.css's own header comment already names "the join/land departure boards" in that list — the decision exists, only the per-declaration markers are missing. So: mark them, do not tokenise them.

And the ration is not violated, because it was never app-wide: "max one board surface per screen". Login has one — itself. Dark Home has one — its hero.

§8 The toggle

/settings is user-scoped and already exists. ADR-0133 §7 rejected a theme toggle there in July for one stated reason — "a switch that does nothing is worse than a thin page" — which was true while the remap was inert and expires the moment it is not.

/settings — light The real page as it ships today; the theme control joins it as a third section.

/settings — dark ships Same page, remapped.

Three rungs — מערכת / בהיר / כהה — and מערכת is the default. Two rungs would force a shipped default and a dark-mode phone would open light. system is also the only honest state for someone who has never chosen, and it must keep tracking, so the stored value is the pick and the resolved theme is computed from it plus a live matchMedia listener.

It stores on the device, and that is the design rather than the shortcut. The theme has to be on <html> before first paint or the app flashes light — which a server-stored preference structurally cannot do, since it arrives a round trip after the first frame and would change the theme after paint. A local store is load-bearing even if a server copy existed, so the server copy would be pure duplication with a flash attached. It is also the honest model: theme is a property of the screen you are holding — a phone at night and a laptop in daylight legitimately disagree — where avatarHue, the stored-preference precedent, is a property of the person.

Two things that must follow the resolved theme and are easy to miss: <meta name="theme-color">, hardcoded #1B2A4A today, which paints the browser's own chrome bar; and the Map's cloud style, already plumbed for exactly this (MAP_THEME + readMapsConfig(env, theme), ADR-0121 §11) — and safe from §4's no-re-instantiate rule for free, because Map.tsx latches its config at mount.
