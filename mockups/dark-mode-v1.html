Dark mode — the ration, the two fixed screens, and the toggle

The colour work is done and inert: tokens.css carries a complete :root[data-theme='dark'] remap and nothing in the tree sets data-theme. So this file is not about picking colours — it is about the two things a token flip cannot answer. §1 asks what carries the board's one-loud-element ration when the whole app is dark. §2 asks whether /login and /join/:token — full-screen theme-fixed boards today — should theme at all. §3 places the toggle. §6 measures the rendered pixels rather than the table someone typed.

§1 The ration: what makes the board loud when everything is dark

In light mode the board is loud by contrast of luminance against paper — it is the only dark thing on the screen. Dark mode deletes that mechanism. The remap's answer (design-language, "Dark mode readiness") is that the board keeps hierarchy by owning the darkest surface while cards sit on lighter dark ones, and elevation flips to "lighter = closer". Three candidates against the same body, so the claim can be looked at instead of believed.

D — a neutral hairline rejected The edge that spends no budget (--line, light @ .10). Rejected on a different ground: a light rim on the app's lowest plane says "raised", which is the opposite of what the board now is. It buys objecthood by lying about elevation.

What the render corrected. design-language's stated dark-mode mechanism is that the board "owns the darkest surface … while cards sit on lighter dark surfaces". Measured on the pixels above, that step is ΔL* 2.6 — against ΔL* 84.8 in light. It is not a visible edge, and A shows it: the board has no boundary against the body. So the doc's mechanism is real in the token table and does essentially no work on screen. That is worth writing down, because the number in the table looks like an answer.
What actually carries it: amber density. Look at A again. The clock, the הבא בתור label, the countdown numeral, the platform numeral, the progress bar, the live blip — every amber thing on the screen is inside the board. The eye goes straight there with no luminance edge involved at all. And the hierarchy inverts rather than weakening: in light the board is the object and the cards are quiet; in dark the cards are the objects and the board is the field they sit above. That inversion is not a defect to correct. It is closer to what the board is — the thing that speaks, not a thing you pick up — and it is exactly why B and D are both wrong: each restores an edge the surface no longer needs.
The rule this sharpens, and it is a real tightening. In light mode a stray amber accent elsewhere on the screen is survivable, because the board's darkness still separates it. In dark mode amber density IS the ration — so amber spent anywhere else is taken directly out of the board's prominence, with nothing left to compensate. "Amber = time & commitment" stops being a semantic rule and becomes a hierarchy rule. The event cards' amber hard-edge and mono times are the tolerated case (small, static, non-glowing); anything larger or glowing is not, and there is no longer a second channel that would have hidden the mistake.
Two things the render exposed that were not on the brief. (1) The header changes plane. Dark --indigo #131F38 is lighter than the body ground, so the always-dark chrome stops being "the dark bar at the top" and becomes a raised band — fine under "lighter = closer", but it means mode identity is carried by hue alone in dark mode, which §1b checks rather than assumes. (2) --faint on --card measures 2.63:1 in the LIGHT theme — under its own AA-large floor, a pre-existing light-mode defect that dark mode fixes by accident (4.76:1). Pulling that thread is what turned up §5b, and it is the reason this session audited both themes rather than only the new one.

§1b Mode identity survives the remap (ADR-0028, non-negotiable)

Mode must be readable from the chrome alone, before any content, via at least two channels. Light mode gets that almost for free — trip chrome is dark indigo, plan chrome is light paper, so luminance alone separates them. Dark mode deletes that channel too: both chromes are now dark. Drawn, because "it still works" is a claim about a rendered screen.

Plan · dark, AS THE REMAP SHIPS TODAY defect The hero fills from --plan-deep, whose dark value was chosen "used as text — lighter on dark". It is also a fill at two sites. Result: L* 68 — the brightest surface in the entire dark app, in the mode design-language calls calm, with its own ink at 2.16:1.

The bar is "completely different at a glance", not "two channels technically survive". ADR-0028's rule is chrome colour + mode pill + texture — hue, glyph and pattern, all of which survive a luminance remap. But light mode clears that bar by a mile for a reason the rule never names: trip chrome is dark and plan chrome is light, so the two modes differ in luminance before you notice anything else. Dark mode deletes that channel, and the remap as shipped does not just fail to replace it — it inverts it, making plan the loud mode and trip the quiet one.
So plan's hero gets surface tokens, exactly as trip's does. The asymmetry is the root cause: the board has dedicated surface tokens (--board/--board-2) while the plan hero has always been painting itself out of the accent tokens (--plan-deep/--plan). An accent brightens on dark; a surface must not. Adding --plan-surface/--plan-surface-2 — light values byte-identical to today, dark values a deep violet night surface (#2A2158/#332866, L* 17–21, ink at 12.7:1) — keeps dark mode a token remap, restores plan's calm, and gives the two modes their luminance channel back the right way round: trip = near-black with amber inside it, plan = a lifted violet slab with white on it. Unmistakable at a glance, which is the actual requirement.

§2 The convergence: /login and /join beside a dark app

Both screens are full-screen departure boards today, deliberately — the pre-login brand impression, and design-language's one stated exception to the ration ("the pre-login landing teaser is the one marketing exception"). Their ground is --board-2 → --board, which does re-map, so they already shift by the remap's few L* without anyone wiring anything. The question is whether they should do more than that.

The rejected alternative: /login themes rejected What "these screens theme" actually means — a light login, because a screen that themes has to have a light state. The brand impression is gone, and ADR-0142/0143's set pieces (the anticipation glow, the perforation, the stamp) now need a second choreography and a second device pass.

The answer: they do not theme, and the argument is not "there is no preference to read". A locally-stored pick is readable on /login (§3), so that argument dissolves. The real one is that these two screens are already at the dark end of the axis — theming them means giving them a light state they have never had and do not want, and the only alternative reading ("theming" = the ground goes a few L* darker) is what the tokens already do for free. They are the same surface class as the indigo header, the dormant board and the boarding-pass ticket: always-dark chrome, every one of which the U-08 sweep already marked fixed:. App.css's own header comment already names "the join/land departure boards" in that list — the decision exists, only the per-declaration markers are missing. So: mark them, don't tokenize them.
And the ration is not violated, because the ration was never app-wide. "Max one board surface per screen." Login has one — itself. Dark Home has one — its hero. Two different screens both being dark has never been the rule. What dark mode ends is the implicit second thing the rule bought (a board is recognisable because everything around it is paper), and §1 is where that mechanism gets restated rather than lost.

Boot — dark ships ADR-0105's night board, amber lit, full-bleed. One rule, not a component: the ground becomes the same --board-2 → --board gradient /login uses, so boot→login is one continuous surface in dark.

It stores on the device, and that is the design rather than the shortcut. The theme has to be on <html> before first paint or the app flashes light — which a server-stored preference structurally cannot do, since it arrives a round trip after the first frame and would change the theme after paint. A local store is therefore load-bearing even if a server copy existed, so the server copy would be pure duplication with a flash attached. It is also the honest model: theme is a property of the screen you are holding — a phone at night and a laptop in daylight legitimately disagree — where avatarHue, the stored-preference precedent, is a property of the person.
Three rungs, and מערכת is the default. Two rungs would force a shipped default, and a dark-mode phone would open light. system is also the only honest state for a user who has never chosen — and it must keep tracking, so the stored value is the pick (system|light|dark) and the resolved theme is computed from it plus a live matchMedia listener. Note what else has to follow the resolved theme and is easy to forget: <meta name="theme-color"> (hardcoded #1B2A4A today — it paints the browser's own chrome bar), and the Map's cloud style, which is already plumbed for exactly this (MAP_THEME + readMapsConfig(env, theme), ADR-0121 §11).

§5 The convention that is wrong, and the ten sites it breaks

App.css and screens.css both open with the same recorded rule from the U-08 sweep: "ink that rides a semantic FILL — the fill re-maps, the on-fill ink must not." That rule is true for a fill that keeps its polarity and false for every mid-tone semantic fill, because those were brightened for text legibility on dark grounds — which pushes them straight past the point where white ink works. Nobody could see it: the remap has never been rendered.

כפי שנשלח היום

Dark, today broken Four real controls. The first is background: var(--ink) with #fff — 1.20:1, white on near-white. It is the selected state of .choice-pill, a control in the app's forms.

עם ‎--on-fill

Dark, fixed ships 13.49 · 7.03 · 5.55 · 6.34. A brightened fill takes dark ink — which is what dark-mode fills do everywhere, and what --cta-text has been doing alone in this codebase since ADR-0028.

בהיר — ללא שינוי

Light — byte-identical --on-fill is #FFFFFF in light, so every one of these renders exactly as it does today. The change is invisible in light mode by construction, which is what makes it safe.

One pair, generalised from the one that already existed. Rule 8 says find the one-off doing this job and extend it rather than adding a second. That one-off is --cta/--cta-text — ADR-0028 already wrote "CTA is a pair … they flip together (dark: light button, dark text)", and then applied the insight to exactly one token. --on-fill is that same pair's ink, named for every fill: #FFFFFF light, #12203A dark (the value --cta-text already carries). --cta-text becomes an alias of it. --on-amber takes the other case — the one fill that is light in both themes, so its ink stays dark in both.
The one fill that must NOT join the family: --indigo. It is always-dark chrome, so it keeps light ink in both themes — dark ink on it measures 1.01:1. This is the same distinction §2 draws for /login and /join, and it is the line the whole "fixed:" marker convention exists to record: always-dark chrome keeps light ink; a re-mapping fill flips its ink with it. Those are two different rules that the sweep wrote down as one.

§5b And the light theme has four failures of its own

The reconciliation was supposed to be about dark mode. Auditing both themes together turned the result inside out: text contrast fails four times in the SHIPPED light theme and zero times in dark — the dark remap brightened everything and fixed them by accident. Symmetrically, on-fill ink fails ten times in dark and zero times in light. Each theme has been hiding the other's bug class.

Light, corrected ships 5.49 · 4.50 · 3.03. All three are scalar darkenings along the same RGB ray, so hue and saturation are mathematically untouched — the amber is still the same amber, just deep enough to read.

Why --amber itself is NOT touched. The signature amber #E9A63C lives on the dark board, where it measures 8.51:1. Only --amber-deep — amber's paper variant, carrying 10–12.5px bold times and labels across zone chips, leg badges, the glance rail and the tz-shift pill — moves. So the product's signature colour is unchanged and the rule-4 budget is untouched: this is the same hue at a legible depth, not a new one.

The pattern worth keeping. Both bug classes have one shape — a token whose value was chosen for one of its two roles. --amber-deep was chosen as an accent and used as small text. --plan-deep was remapped as text and used as a fill. The ten on-fill sites inherited a rule written for amber and applied to everything. Each time the fix is the same: give the second role its own token, which is what --amber-ink already did once and nobody generalised.

§6 Measured off the rendered pixels

Computed at load from getComputedStyle on the frames above — not from a table anyone typed. L* is CIE lightness; ratios are WCAG 2.x contrast. These are the floor, not the verdict: ADR-0125 is this repo's own precedent for a palette that measured fine and read as one hue on a real screen, and the map epic has had four device corrections of things that were derived, tested and wrong. The device pass is still owed.
